Abstract

A range scan records more than where a surface is: every measured ray also states where the surface is not. Most implicit reconstruction methods discard this, treating the scan as an unordered point cloud. We instead take the sensor origin of every point as input and fit a continuous 1-Lipschitz implicit field directly to the rays, supervising the first intersection found by differentiable sphere tracing rather than an unsigned distance to the points. Starting from a closed-form field enclosing the scene, depth supervision carves the surface inward toward the observations. The Lipschitz constraint turns visibility into a geometric certificate: positive field values along a measured ray guarantee a ball of empty space around it, so one-dimensional ray evidence becomes a three-dimensional free-space guarantee. The result is robust to acquisition noise, outliers and sparse sampling, and avoids hallucinating surfaces in regions the sensor has observed to be empty.

Approach

Input

\mathcal{D} = \{(\mathbf{c}_i, \mathbf{p}_i)\}_{i=1}^{N}

\begin{alignedat}{2} &\mathbf{c}_i \in \mathbb{R}^3 \quad && \text{acquisition center} \\ &\mathbf{p}_i \in \mathbb{R}^3 \quad && \text{noisy measurement of } \mathcal{S}^\star \\ &\mathbf{d}_i = (\mathbf{p}_i - \mathbf{c}_i) / \lVert \mathbf{p}_i - \mathbf{c}_i \rVert \quad && \text{ray direction} \end{alignedat}

Goal

\text{reconstruct } \mathcal{S}^\star \text{ as } f_\theta^{-1}(0)

Depth regression along rays

\begin{aligned} \min_{\theta} \;\; & \frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N} \rho\bigl(t_i(\theta) - \lVert \mathbf{p}_i - \mathbf{c}_i \rVert\bigr) \\ \text{s.t.} \;\; & t_i(\theta) = \inf\,\{t \ge 0 : f_\theta(\mathbf{c}_i + t\,\mathbf{d}_i) = 0\} \end{aligned}

\begin{alignedat}{2} &\bullet\;\; f_\theta \quad && \text{1-Lipschitz MLP} \\ &\bullet\;\; \rho \quad && \text{robust penalty} \end{alignedat}

Training

Reconstruction of the SRB Gargoyle scene in a clean setting. We use the positional encoding parameter L as a simple knob to study the fidelity–robustness trade-off: increasing L gives more fitting capacity but less robustness to corruption. Ours (L=6) captures finer geometric details.

Results

Noise

Reconstruction of the SRB Anchor scene with increasing scanner noise levels. The lower fitting capacity of Ours (L=0) makes it more robust to noise, while Ours (L=2) offers a good compromise at intermediate noise levels.

Outliers

Reconstruction of the SRB Anchor scene with increasing outlier ratios (0%, 5%, 10%, 20%) relative to the number of clean points. By enforcing visibility constraints and using a Huber loss, our method remains robust to outliers.

ARKitScenes

Sofa reconstruction from real-world LiDAR scans captured with an iPad Pro. Ours (L=2) yields a much more faithful reconstruction than DiGS and DiffCD: it is not oversmoothed and recovers fine details such as the cushions.
